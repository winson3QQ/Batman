## 1. `prov-ac-flap-254` asserts the P3 rule

**Premise.** A trial whose helper keeps exiting is not commit-worthy. The deadline revert removes it even while it is held.

**Why this is deterministic** (v1 A, verified):
- The helper never lives the 60 s that `provd-stable` needs.
- So every poll's why is the (a) or the (b) reason, and HELD cannot occur.

**Flow** (shaped like `prov-ac-radio1off-254.sh`):
1. `pr_begin 3000`, then precondition `ble-ok`, then `pr_ota_b "$PR_V.dirty.wbox-helper-flap" B`.
2. Poll every 10 s, in the trial boot, until 30 s before the trial's own deadline (`$RUNDIR/autocommit.deadline`).
   - Log every `autocommit.why` sample to the unit log.
   - Kept from the old unit: the mutant is live (at least 2 `WBOX-helper-flap-f4703b1d: exiting` lines) and the health file is fresh (F14).
3. Call `pr_wait_reverted`. It FAILs on HELD, renews the lease on a deferral, and reports INVALID on a panic. It also re-finds the node after the revert (`prov-254.sh:417-450`).
4. Judge with `acflap_judge <samples> <PR_REVERT_LINE>`:
   - at least one sample must *contain* `svc batman-provhelper down`; if none does, FAIL "(a) never observed";
   - the revert line must contain (a) or `batman-provd not stable`.
5. `pr_end revert`.

**Negative controls**
- Offline: `scripts/dv/test-acflap-254.sh` feeds `acflap_judge` with synthetic inputs, labelled as such:
  - (a) samples plus an (a) revert line → PASS;
  - (b)-only samples → FAIL;
  - a revert reason of `mesh not joined` → FAIL;
  - no samples → FAIL.
- On the device: wsl.35 FAILed under the old judgement (the evidence above). The new unit must PASS on wsl.36 with its samples logged.

**E-H5 #2** (`254-h23-harness.md:989`): the old dry-run arm's timed run moves unchanged into `prov-autocommit-p3-254`, on its healthy trial.

## 4. Slot commands: guarded, prechecked, or allowlisted with a reason

**The rule.** Every command in scope that reboots, rolls back or commits a node is one of:
- **guarded**, through `slot-guard-254.sh`;
- **prechecked**: in the same ssh command, a read-only tryboot GET must read "clear" before the plain reboot;
- **allowlisted**, with a reason.

The lint enforces this. Out of scope: product-initiated reboots and killpoints.

**Pi 3 policy** (v3 A m1, B m4: v3's "Pi 3 sites stay unconverted" was inconsistent, because the precheck needs the same word 5 as the guard).
- The fleet's existing rule is that both SoCs must answer the tryboot GET: `chk_trybootget_209`, `daily-validation.sh:745-750`. `batman-slot apply` already refuses on a Pi 3 that does not.
- The guards and the precheck rely on that rule. A Pi 3 whose firmware does not answer word 5 makes them fail closed (WRONG-STATE, or FAIL "not answered"). That is the same verdict trybootget-209 gives that node.
- ⚠️ Word 5 has never been captured on a Pi 3 (`254-v16-group1.md:59`). Recorded as an open line in #254; the bench has no Pi 3.
- No site is exempted for its SoC. Exemptions are for what the site does.

### 4.1 Guard additions (`slot-guard-254.sh`)

**`commit_guarded <boot8> <slot>`**
- Same boot, same slot, and in a trial (is-trial rc 0) → `batman-slot commit` → `SENT <output>`.
- Anything else → `WRONG-STATE`, and no commit.
- No DISARM: a commit does not reboot.

**`reboot_guarded <boot8> <slot> <trial> <reason> [extra] [pre]`** (v3 A m8)
- `_sg_cmd` today takes `b a t why x act` (`:40`). `<pre>` becomes its 7th parameter, and the wrapper maps the public 6th argument onto it.
- An `extra` of `-` maps to `true`.
- `<pre>` runs after every check passed. Its output goes to `logger -t slot-guard`.
- Today's ordering is kept: the reboot follows even when `<pre>` fails. The answer is then `PRE-FAILED SENT`, and the caller FAILs.
- On `WRONG-STATE`, `<pre>` never runs.

**DISARM as a second writer of the product's tryboot flag**
- It clears the flag only on a committed node, right before a reboot.
- A failed clear means no reboot.

### 4.3 The lint

**Scope.** `scripts/dv`, `scripts/node`, `scripts/fault-injection.sh`, `scripts/ab-selftest.sh`, `scripts/negctl-old-slot-254.sh`, `scripts/daily-validation.sh`. Excluded: `test-*.sh`, `slot-guard-254.sh`, and the inventory file (v3 A M2).

**Lint (1): slot commands.** Today's regex plus the `reboot` verb, `"$BS"` and quoted paths: `(batman-slot["']?|"\$BS")[[:space:]]+["']?(rollback|commit|reboot)` and `batman-autocommit[[:space:]]+["']?release`. Every hit must match an allowlist row. On b885a2c the ten `batman-slot reboot committed` host sites of rows 1–10 are hits with no row, so they FAIL.

**Lint (2): pinned guarded sites.** Today's pinned argument table, extended:
- the new `reboot_guarded` / `commit_guarded` rows, with `<pre>`;
- **plus the precheck line inside `reboot_and_wait`** (v3 B m8, A M2), so removing the precheck FAILs.

**Lint (3): plain reboots.** Every hit of `\breboot\b|sysrq-trigger`, minus comment lines, must match a row of `scripts/dv/data/slot-sites.tsv`. The guard words do not hit this regex.
- A row is: file, the **exact** trimmed line text, the **exact** hit count, and a group: `allowlisted:<reason>`, `prechecked` (the trigger inside `reboot_and_wait` and its callers' arguments) or `text`.
- Matching is exact, not by substring, so a copied reboot line adds a hit and FAILs the count (v3 A M2, B m8).
- A hit with no row, a row with no hit, or a count that differs is a FAIL. So is an empty inventory or zero hits.
- 172 hits today (v3 A, measured). The inventory is generated once by a helper script, `scripts/dv/gen-slot-sites.sh --draft`, and every non-`text` row is reviewed by hand.

**Negative control** (b885a2c):
- lint (1) names rows 1–10;
- lint (2) names every new pin (rows 11–13, 15, 19, the precheck);
- lint (3) names the plain-reboot hits of the converted rows, because their lines are no longer in the inventory (row 19 `sync; reboot`; rows 36–38 on the node).

The expected FAIL set is generated from the inventory and the pin table, not written by hand. After the change all three PASS.

## 9. Security

**Assets:** control of the two bench nodes (slot, boot, p5 identity).

**Actors:**
- **Our own mistakes:** an armed reboot, an unmount of a product write, a wipe followed by a refusal, a test that cannot fail. Each is addressed above:
  - the guards fail closed;
  - `<pre>` keeps today's ordering;
  - only harness mounts are touched;
  - the CI test fails on zero rows or on a mutant that passes.
- **A local unprivileged host process:** same trust as today. The patch files and the inventory are repo content, reviewed like code. A host-derived mutant runs only in a dry-run bind on the node.
- **A compromised node:** nothing new is trusted from node output. The DISARM, `<pre>` and commit run only after the node-side checks pass.

**Attack-surface delta:**
- no new port, node env or secret;
- no test seam in the node script: the p5 test runs main and the fix unmodified in a mount namespace (§3.2);
- `<pre>` is a fixed string from the harness source.

**Privilege:**
- The node-side restore runs as root, as today.
- It unmounts only mountpoints under `/tmp/run/batman-dv/` and the fixed `/mnt/dv-p5`, matched on the /proc/mounts entry.
- A non-root process cannot create a mount there.

**Failure mode:** fail-closed throughout.

**Secrets:** none touched by the restore. **Dead-man trade-off** (v3 A m5): today the dead-man reboots even with the flag armed, which ends the IRK exposure but may boot a trial slot. Now a failed clear means no reboot, so a bench test IRK stays live in tmpfs until a person reboots the node. The bench IRKs are test keys, so slot safety is preferred. The `deadman: tryboot not cleared` line and the kept markers make the case visible, and the next run's precondition stops on it.
